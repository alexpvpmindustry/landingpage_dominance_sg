SmartManu AI: Singapore's Manufacturing Intelligence Revolution

Date: February 26, 2026

Southeast Asia's manufacturing sector is undergoing a quiet transformation, and a Singapore startup is leading the charge. SmartManu AI, founded in 2024 by former engineers from Singapore's Agency for Science, Technology and Research (A*STAR), is bringing artificial intelligence to factory floors across the region—helping manufacturers predict equipment failures before they happen and optimize production in real-time.

The Manufacturing Efficiency Challenge

Factories across Southeast Asia face a common challenge: how to maximize productivity while minimizing costly unplanned downtime. Traditional maintenance approaches—either reactive repairs after breakdowns or scheduled preventive maintenance—leave money on the table. Reactive maintenance means unexpected shutdowns that can cost thousands per hour, while preventive maintenance often replaces components too early, wasting resources.

"The manufacturing industry has been collecting data for decades, but most of it sits in silos or isn't analysed effectively," explained Dr. Marcus Tan, SmartManu AI's founder and chief executive officer. "Our AI platform connects to existing sensors and machinery, then uses machine learning to identify patterns that human engineers would never spot—predicting failures weeks before they occur."

How SmartManu's AI Works

The SmartManu platform integrates with existing industrial equipment through a combination of vibration sensors, temperature monitors, and equipment control systems. The AI continuously analyses data streams, building models that understand what "normal" operation looks like for each piece of machinery.

What makes SmartManu distinctive is its ability to work with both modern IoT-enabled equipment and older legacy machinery common in Southeast Asian factories. The platform uses edge computing to process data locally, reducing latency and ensuring operations continue even during connectivity disruptions.

The system provides factory managers with a dashboard showing real-time health scores for critical equipment, predicted failure timelines, and recommended maintenance actions. It also optimizes production schedules by factoring in equipment wear patterns, ensuring high-wear machines get priority during low-production periods.

Singapore's Manufacturing Ambitions

SmartManu's emergence aligns with Singapore's ambitions to transform its manufacturing sector. The city-state's "Smart Nation" initiative extends to industrial transformation, with the government offering incentives for companies adopting advanced technologies.

"Singapore's manufacturing sector contributes about 20% of our GDP, and the government recognises that AI is key to staying competitive," noted Dr. Tan. "We've received strong support from Enterprise Singapore, and our pilot programs with local manufacturers have demonstrated clear ROI."

The startup has partnered with several Singapore-based electronics and precision engineering manufacturers, achieving average reductions in unplanned downtime of 35% and extending equipment lifespan by 20%. These results have attracted attention from manufacturers across the region.

Expanding Across Southeast Asia

While Singapore serves as SmartManu's home base and proving ground, the company is now expanding to Vietnam, Thailand, and Indonesia—countries with rapidly growing manufacturing sectors hungry for efficiency improvements.

"Each market has its unique characteristics," explained Ms. Priya Sharma, SmartManu's head of expansion. "In Vietnam, we're working with new factories being built with Industry 4.0 in mind. In Thailand and Indonesia, we're often retrofitting older facilities. Our platform adapts to both scenarios."

The company recently closed a US$12 million Series A funding round led by Sequoia Southeast Asia, with participation from existing investors. The funding will support expansion into new markets and development of specialized AI models for different manufacturing verticals, including food processing, pharmaceuticals, and automotive parts.

The Future of Intelligent Manufacturing

SmartManu's vision extends beyond predictive maintenance. The company is developing AI systems that can autonomously optimise entire production lines—adjusting parameters in real-time to maximize throughput while minimizing waste and energy consumption.

"We're moving toward truly autonomous manufacturing," Dr. Tan explained. "Our AI will eventually be able to make micro-adjustments to production parameters every few seconds, something no human engineer could do. This is the future of competitive manufacturing."

As Southeast Asia continues its manufacturing ascent, companies like SmartManu AI represent a new generation of regional startups applying cutting-edge AI to real-world industrial challenges. For Singapore, the startup exemplifies how the city-state is positioning itself not just as a hub for AI research, but as a centre for AI-powered industrial innovation.
