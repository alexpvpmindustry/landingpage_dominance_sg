Singapore Primary Schools Embrace AI Under Guard Rails

Date: April 20, 2026

Singapore's youngest learners are stepping into the world of artificial intelligence — but with careful guard rails in place. The Ministry of Education (MOE) has begun introducing AI tools in primary schools, specifically for students from Primary 4 onwards, under strict supervision and with carefully selected educational platforms.

The Approach: Guard Rails First, AI Second

Education Minister Desmond Lee announced earlier in 2026 that AI would be gradually introduced from Primary 4, with "low exposure" and priority given to learning fundamentals. This cautious approach reflects a broader recognition that while AI skills are increasingly essential, young children need protection from the risks associated with unmoderated AI tools.

"As AI becomes increasingly pervasive, it is better that schools teach pupils to use AI well rather than leave it to chance and the open internet," said Ms Liew Wei Li, Director-General of Education at MOE, in a LinkedIn post addressing parental concerns.

Teacher-Led AI: The SchoolAI Platform

At the forefront of this initiative is SchoolAI, an AI-powered educational platform designed specifically for Singapore's classrooms. Teachers like a 45-year-old English language teacher in Singapore have been using it for the past three years to help Primary 5 and 6 students write better compositions.

What sets SchoolAI apart is its Socratic approach. Rather than providing students with ready-made answers, the AI prompts them with questions that encourage deeper thinking about character dilemmas and plot twists. "The AI won't write the story for them, but it prompts them to ask if this is the choice the character would make, and adds layers to the story," the teacher explained.

The platform also serves as a teaching assistant that can provide one-on-one guidance — something that isn't practical for teachers managing a full classroom. It flags students who are struggling or going off-task, allowing teachers to intervene where they're needed most.

Parent Concerns: Navigating the AI Debate

Not all parents are comfortable with AI entering the primary school classroom. Some worry about overreliance on technology and its impact on children's critical thinking skills. Others are already grappling with limiting screen time from an early age, making them hesitant about increased technology use for learning.

One parent spoke of her shock when her Primary 5 son was assigned homework requiring the use of ChatGPT at home. "I restrict his access to devices at home, and I was shocked that he was asked to use AI. He had never used it before and was unsure of what to do," she said.

Cybersecurity consultant Mr Ramesh Kumar, who has three children in primary school, takes a more nuanced view. His twins use Geniebook, an online learning platform with AI algorithms that customize learning. "These models have guard rails, which permit what you can or cannot say, what you can and cannot do," he noted.

What's Being Protected

MOE has implemented several safeguards for AI use in primary schools:

  • Restricted Tools: Students only access AI tools developed by MOE through the Singapore Student Learning Space (SLS), not general-purpose AI like ChatGPT
  • Teacher Supervision: All AI use in primary schools requires direct teacher oversight
  • Controlled Responses: AI chatbots are programmed not to give immediate answers, not to allow students to stray from topics, and to limit access to only lesson-related information
  • Structured Learning: Students learn how AI works and how to use it responsibly, alongside understanding why the learning process — including struggling and persevering — matters

The Road Ahead

By the end of their primary school education, Singapore students will have had opportunities to learn about AI — becoming aware of its presence, understanding the risks posed by misinformation, and recognizing that AI output can be inaccurate.

MOE has committed to providing parents with more resources to guide their children's use of AI, recognizing that the partnership between schools and families is crucial in navigating this technological shift.

For now, Singapore's approach represents a carefully calibrated balancing act — embracing the benefits of AI education while building safeguards that protect young minds from its potential pitfalls.
