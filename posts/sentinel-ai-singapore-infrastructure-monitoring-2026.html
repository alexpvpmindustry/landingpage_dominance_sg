Sentinel AI: Singapore Startup Revolutionising Infrastructure Monitoring with Predictive AI

Date: March 22, 2026

Singapore's infrastructure is the backbone of the city-state's global competitiveness—from the sprawling MRT network to the iconic Marina Bay Sands, from residential HDB blocks to the extensive network of bridges and tunnels. Maintaining these assets in a tropical climate that subjects buildings to year-round humidity and seasonal flooding is a massive undertaking. Enter Sentinel AI, a Singapore startup that is fundamentally changing how the nation approaches infrastructure maintenance through artificial intelligence.

The Problem with Reactive Maintenance

Traditionally, infrastructure maintenance has been largely reactive—problems are fixed after they occur. This approach works reasonably well but comes with significant costs. Unexpected failures disrupt millions of daily commuters, create safety hazards, and often require more expensive emergency repairs than planned maintenance would.

"Singapore's infrastructure is world-class, but maintaining that standard requires looking beyond traditional methods," explains Dr. Rachel Tan, founder and CEO of Sentinel AI. "Our roads, buildings, and transport systems generate enormous amounts of data every day. We're using AI to make sense of that data and predict problems before they become visible to the naked eye."

How Sentinel AI Works

Sentinel AI's platform integrates with existing sensor networks, CCTV systems, and building management systems to create a comprehensive view of infrastructure health. The company's proprietary machine learning models analyze patterns that human inspectors might miss—subtle vibrations in bridges, micro-cracks in building foundations, or unusual wear patterns in rail tracks.

The system assigns risk scores to different infrastructure elements, alerting maintenance teams when intervention is recommended. What makes Sentinel AI particularly powerful is its ability to learn from historical data—understanding how Singapore's climate affects different materials, how heavy rainfall impacts drainage systems, and how aging structures behave over time.

Real-World Impact

The technology is already being deployed across several high-profile Singapore projects. The Land Transport Authority (LTA) has partnered with Sentinel AI to monitor sections of the Downtown Line, using the system to predict track wear and optimize maintenance schedules. Early results show a 35% reduction in unexpected track failures since deployment began in late 2025.

Housing & Development Board (HDB) estates have also begun rolling out Sentinel AI's building monitoring capabilities. In a pilot program across 50 blocks in Tengah new town, the system detected early signs of facade degradation in three buildings that would have required major repairs within two years if left unchecked. The timely intervention saved an estimated S$4.5 million in potential repair costs.

"The beauty of predictive maintenance is that it's always cheaper to fix something early," notes Dr. Tan. "Our models don't just tell you there's a problem—they tell you exactly when you need to address it and how urgent it is. This helps infrastructure managers allocate budgets more effectively."

Addressing Singapore's Unique Challenges

Singapore presents specific infrastructure challenges that Sentinel AI's models are designed to address. The city's tropical climate means structures face constant exposure to humidity, which accelerates corrosion in metal components and promotes mold growth in buildings. Seasonal thunderstorms create flooding risks, while the heavily urbanized environment means that even minor infrastructure disruptions can affect thousands of people.

The startup has built climate-specific models trained on Singapore's environmental conditions. These include predicting how the combination of high humidity and air conditioning condensation affects building exteriors, how thermal expansion from daily temperature cycles impacts bridge joints, and how underground MRT tunnels behave during heavy rainy seasons.

Growing Beyond Singapore

While Sentinel AI is currently focused on the Singapore market, the company has ambitious regional expansion plans. Neighboring Southeast Asian cities face similar infrastructure challenges but often with fewer resources for comprehensive monitoring. The company's technology is particularly well-suited for rapidly urbanizing cities in the region that need to stretch maintenance budgets further.

Sentinel AI has already secured pilot partnerships in Malaysia and Vietnam, with plans to expand to Indonesia and Thailand by 2027. The startup recently closed a Series B funding round of S$28 million, led by Sequoia Capital Southeast Asia, with participation from local investors including Temasek-linked Vertex Ventures.

The Future of Smart Infrastructure

Looking ahead, Sentinel AI is developing next-generation capabilities that will integrate even more data sources. The company is working on incorporating drone-based inspection data, satellite imagery, and even crowd-sourced reports from citizens via a dedicated mobile app. The goal is to create a comprehensive "infrastructure health" platform that can monitor everything from individual building components to entire districts.

As Singapore continues its Smart Nation journey, predictive AI maintenance is likely to become standard practice across government infrastructure management. With Sentinel AI leading the charge, the city-state is positioning itself as a global model for infrastructure resilience—one where problems are anticipated rather than reacted to.

For Singaporeans, this means fewer unexpected train breakdowns, safer buildings, and more efficient use of public funds. For the broader Southeast Asian region, Sentinel AI's success offers a template for how AI can help cities manage infrastructure more sustainably as urban populations grow.
