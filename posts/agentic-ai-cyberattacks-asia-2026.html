Agentic AI: The New Frontier of Cyberattacks Sweeping Asia

Date: March 24, 2026

In the shadows of Southeast Asia's rapidly expanding digital economy, a new and terrifying reality is emerging. Cyberattacks are no longer the slow, methodical operations of the past—instead, AI-powered agents are executing sophisticated attacks at speeds that leave human defenders far behind. Security experts warn this marks a fundamental shift in the threat landscape, one that demands an equally powerful response.

"Not How Humans Act"

Reuben Koh, a cybersecurity expert based in Singapore, has seen the future of hacking—and it doesn't look human. "We know that AI agents are being utilised in this manner because no human would do it that way," he told Tech in Asia. The patterns are unmistakable: attacks that unfold in hours instead of days, simultaneous penetration of multiple network layers, and exploitation strategies that evolve in real-time.

For decades, coordinated cyberattacks followed a familiar playbook. Attackers would gain initial access, then carefully move through a network over days or weeks, meticulously avoiding detection. Each step required human decision-making, planning, and execution. Those days are over.

The Rise of Autonomous Attack Agents

Today's AI-driven attacks operate fundamentally differently. Autonomous AI agents now handle nearly every aspect of a cyberattack—from initial reconnaissance to lateral movement, privilege escalation, and data exfiltration. Human attackers now step in only for strategic decisions, essentially functioning as commanders overseeing an army of digital soldiers that never sleep, never tire, and never make mistakes from fatigue.

In Singapore alone, the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) reported a 47% increase in cyber incidents in 2025 compared to the previous year. While not all of these can be attributed to AI-powered attacks, security analysts believe the proportion is growing rapidly. The city-state's position as a regional financial and technology hub makes it an attractive target for increasingly sophisticated threat actors.

Fighting Fire with Fire

The solution, experts say, requires embracing the same technology that's being used against us. "The only viable defence is to fight fire with fire," noted one security researcher. This means deploying AI-powered defense systems capable of detecting and responding to autonomous threats at machine speed.

Singapore has been Quick to respond. The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) has been working with local companies to develop AI-driven security solutions that can match the speed and sophistication of agentic AI attacks. These systems use machine learning to identify anomalous patterns, predict attack vectors, and automatically deploy countermeasures.

The Regional Impact

The phenomenon isn't limited to Singapore. Across Asia, organizations are grappling with the new reality of AI-powered threats. Financial institutions in Hong Kong, technology companies in Japan, and government agencies in South Korea have all reported increasingly sophisticated attacks that bear the hallmarks of autonomous AI systems.

The economic implications are significant. According to a report by cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike, the average cost of a data breach in Asia-Pacific reached US$4.2 million in 2025—a figure that's expected to rise as AI-powered attacks become more prevalent. The speed of these attacks means that traditional defense mechanisms, designed for slower human-operated threats, are increasingly inadequate.

The Arms Race Accelerates

What makes agentic AI particularly dangerous is its ability to learn and adapt. Unlike traditional malware, which follows predetermined patterns, AI-powered attack agents can analyze defense responses and modify their tactics in real-time. This creates a continuous arms race where the margin for error grows smaller by the day.

For organizations in Singapore and across Asia, the message is clear: the era of passive cybersecurity is over. Whether you're a multinational corporation or a small startup, the question is no longer if you'll face AI-powered attacks, but when—and whether you'll be ready to respond.

As Singapore continues to position itself as a global AI hub, its ability to defend against these emerging threats will be a critical test of its technological maturity. The stakes have never been higher, and the only way forward is to match the AI threat with AI-powered defense.
