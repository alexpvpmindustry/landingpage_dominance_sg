Bridge Data Centres to Invest $5 Billion in Singapore AI Infrastructure

Date: March 12, 2026

Singapore's position as Asia's leading AI hub just got a massive boost. Bridge Data Centres (BDC), a Bain Capital-backed company, has announced plans to invest up to US$5 billion (approximately S$6.7 billion) in artificial intelligence technology development in Singapore, marking one of the largest infrastructure investments in the city-state's tech sector.

A Revolutionary Investment for AI Infrastructure

The ambitious investment plan will focus on several cutting-edge areas: advanced power architectures, next-generation cooling systems, AI-enabled operations, and energy optimisation for high-density computing environments. Eric Fan, BDC's Chief Executive Officer, emphasized that Singapore is the "primary focus of this initiative."

"We will also recruit technical and R&D talent to support AI-related facilities and innovation," Fan stated, highlighting the company's commitment to building both physical infrastructure and human capital in Singapore.

Singapore's First Floating Hydrogen Power Solution

Among the most innovative initiatives is BDC's development of Singapore's first floating hydrogen power generation solution, being developed in partnership with Concord New Energy. This barge-based structure is designed specifically to power future AI data centres, representing a groundbreaking approach to sustainable computing infrastructure.

The floating hydrogen power system addresses one of the biggest challenges facing AI data centres: the enormous energy demands required to cool high-density computing environments running advanced AI workloads. By harnessing hydrogen power, BDC aims to create a more sustainable and resilient energy solution for Singapore's growing AI ecosystem.

Asia-Wide Ambitions

While Singapore serves as the primary focus, BDC's vision extends across Asia. The company aims to deliver as much as three gigawatts of global capacity by 2030, with expansion plans spanning markets from India to Southeast Asia. This regional approach positions Singapore as the epicenter of BDC's Asian operations.

The global race to build AI infrastructure has sparked an unprecedented boom in data centres, with tech giants and investors pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into facilities packed with advanced chips and sophisticated cooling systems. However, the rapid pace of construction has also raised questions about whether these investments will deliver sufficient economic returns.

Why Singapore?

Singapore's attractiveness as an AI infrastructure hub stems from several key factors: its political stability, world-class connectivity, business-friendly environment, and strategic location in the heart of Southeast Asia. The city-state has also proactively developed policies supporting data centre growth while addressing energy and sustainability concerns.

The government's ongoing efforts to position Singapore as a global AI hub—including initiatives like the National AI Strategy 2.0 and the new AI-focused One Pass—create a supportive ecosystem for companies like BDC to thrive.

What This Means for Singapore's AI Ecosystem

This $5 billion investment represents a significant vote of confidence in Singapore's AI ambitions. The development of advanced cooling systems and AI-optimized infrastructure will attract more AI companies seeking high-performance computing capabilities. Meanwhile, the focus on hydrogen power demonstrates Singapore's commitment to sustainable AI development.

For businesses and researchers in Southeast Asia, this investment means better access to the computing infrastructure needed to develop and deploy AI solutions at scale. The recruitment of R&D talent will also strengthen Singapore's position as a hub for AI innovation and expertise.

As the AI revolution continues to accelerate, infrastructure investments like this will be crucial in determining which cities and nations lead the pack. With BDC's $5 billion commitment, Singapore has just strengthened its claim to be Asia's AI capital.
