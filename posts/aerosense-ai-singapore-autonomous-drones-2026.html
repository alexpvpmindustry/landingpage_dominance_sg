AeroSense AI: Singapore's Autonomous Drone Revolution Transforms Infrastructure Inspection

Date: April 16, 2026

Singapore's skyline is constantly evolving, with new buildings, infrastructure, and megaprojects reshaping the city-state almost monthly. Keeping tabs on this rapid development—and ensuring structural safety—has always been a monumental task. Enter AeroSense AI, a Singapore startup that is fundamentally changing how the city-state monitors its built environment through autonomous AI-powered drones.

The Challenge of Urban Infrastructure Inspection

Singapore's urban landscape presents unique inspection challenges. With over 1,400 high-rise buildings in the Central Business District alone, traditional manual inspection methods are time-consuming, expensive, and sometimes dangerous. Scaffolding and rope access inspections for tall structures can take weeks and cost hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Beyond buildings, Singapore's extensive infrastructure network—including bridges, flyovers, drainage systems, and the MRT rail network—requires constant monitoring for wear and tear. The Housing & Development Board (HDB) manages over 1 million residential units across thousands of estates, each requiring periodic structural assessments.

"We realized that Singapore's infrastructure challenges were perfect for drone technology," explained AeroSense AI's founder and CEO, Dr. Kevin Wong. "But off-the-shelf drones weren't intelligent enough. They needed a brain—something that could not only fly autonomously but also analyze what it sees in real-time."

How AeroSense AI Works

AeroSense AI's platform combines advanced drone hardware with sophisticated machine learning computer vision systems. Their drones are equipped with multi-spectral sensors capable of detecting structural anomalies that are invisible to the naked eye—including micro-cracks, water ingress, corrosion hotspots, and temperature variations indicating potential failures.

The system operates in three stages. First, the AI plans optimal flight paths automatically, taking into account no-fly zones, building geometry, and inspection requirements. Unlike manual drone operations that require skilled pilots, AeroSense drones can be deployed by operators with minimal training.

Second, during flight, the onboard AI processes sensor data in real-time, identifying anomalies and prioritizing inspection zones based on urgency. This means potentially critical issues get immediate attention rather than waiting for post-flight analysis.

Third, the platform generates comprehensive inspection reports automatically, complete with annotated images, severity assessments, and recommended maintenance actions. These reports integrate directly with building management systems, creating a continuous monitoring loop.

Real-World Impact Across Singapore

AeroSense AI has already deployed its technology across several significant Singapore projects. The company partnered with a major construction firm to monitor structural integrity during the construction of a new downtown skyscraper, detecting a potentially catastrophic steel connection issue before it could develop.

Singapore's Land Transport Authority (LTA) has also trialed AeroSense drones for bridge inspections across the island. Traditional bridge inspections required lane closures and teams of engineers working at height. AeroSense drones complete the same inspection in hours without disrupting traffic.

"The HDB has been fantastic," said Dr. Wong. "We're now rolling out monitoring across several estates, checking everything from rooftop conditions to facade integrity. The AI can spot problems that would take human inspectors much longer to find."

Singapore's Smart Nation Vision gets a Lift

AeroSense AI's emergence aligns perfectly with Singapore's Smart Nation initiative. The government's push for digital twins—virtual replicas of physical infrastructure that enable simulation and analysis—relies heavily on accurate, up-to-date data. AeroSense drones feed this data continuously, creating living digital models of Singapore's built environment.

The company has also attracted interest from regional markets. Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia all face similar infrastructure monitoring challenges, and AeroSense is in discussions to expand its operations across Southeast Asia.

The Future of Autonomous Infrastructure

Looking ahead, AeroSense AI plans to expand its capabilities significantly. The company is developing predictive maintenance algorithms that can forecast infrastructure degradation before it occurs, enabling truly preventive maintenance rather than reactive repairs.

They're also exploring integration with Singapore's extensive sensor network, including the island-wide CCTV system and environmental monitors. This would create a comprehensive "nervous system" for Singapore's infrastructure, with AI drones serving as mobile sensors that can be deployed wherever needed.

For Singapore's construction and facilities management sectors, AeroSense AI represents a glimpse into the future—where infrastructure monitoring is continuous, intelligent, and increasingly autonomous. As the city-state continues its ambitious development trajectory, such technologies will be essential for keeping Singapore's built environment safe, efficient, and world-class.
