India's AI Summit 2026: Big Ambitions Meet Hard Realities

Date: March 6, 2026

India's AI Impact Summit 2026 was billed as a coming-of-age moment for the country as it seeks to enter the global artificial intelligence race dominated by the rivalry between the US and China. Instead, the summit presented a far more complex picture—underlining both India's ambition to innovate and provide leadership, and its constraints in terms of infrastructure bottlenecks and dependence on foreign technology.

Billion-Dollar Pledges Signal Serious Intent

Despite logistical hiccups that dampened the summit's sheen—glitches in accreditation, last-minute schedule changes, and chaotic venue logistics—the announcements of deals worth billions of dollars reinforced India's serious intent in the AI space.

Reliance Industries chairman Mukesh Ambani, Asia's richest man, pledged investments of around US$110 billion over the next seven years to build AI and data infrastructure in India. Indian conglomerate Adani Enterprises said it would invest US$100 billion to build renewable-powered, AI-ready data centres by 2035.

Major technology partnerships also emerged: Tata Consultancy Services partnered with OpenAI to build AI infrastructure, while Infosys entered into a partnership with US-based Anthropic to provide AI solutions across telecommunications, financial services, manufacturing, and software development. Meanwhile, Nvidia unveiled tie-ups with three Indian cloud computing providers to provide advanced processors for data centres capable of training and running AI systems.

The Infrastructure Challenge

Behind the headline-grabbing pledges, however, lies a more nuanced reality. India's ambitious AI agenda faces significant infrastructure constraints that could slow execution.

The country remains heavily dependent on foreign technology—particularly US chips from Nvidia and other semiconductor providers—to power its AI ambitions. While Indian startups like Sarvam AI are working on domestically-developed models trained with local datasets to be more culturally attuned to Indian languages and culture, they still face an uphill battle against well-established global players.

"We may have been a little late to the AI race, and we will need to do a lot of catching up," said former minister Rajeev Chandrasekhar. "It is not easy because of the sort of walls and the moats that have been built by the US and the Chinese around their own products and their LLMs. But that is a race that we are going to have to run."

What This Means for Singapore and Southeast Asia

India's AI journey offers valuable lessons for Singapore and the broader Southeast Asian region. The summit highlighted that while capital investment and ambitious pledges are important, execution hinges on addressing fundamental infrastructure needs.

For Singapore, which has positioned itself as Southeast Asia's AI hub, India's progress represents both an opportunity and a competitive dynamic. The city-state's strengths—robust digital infrastructure, government support for AI research, and strategic partnerships with global tech leaders—give it a solid foundation. However, as India accelerates its AI development, regional competition for investment and talent is set to intensify.

The complementary nature of Singapore's AI ecosystem and India's scale could also create opportunities. Singapore's expertise in AI governance, research sophistication, and position as a regional headquarters for multinational tech companies could pair well with India's vast talent pool and growing market.

Looking Ahead

India has undeniably entered the AI race. With 100 million weekly ChatGPT users—making it the second-largest market for the popular AI tool—the country has demonstrated massive consumer appetite for AI technologies. The challenge now is turning that appetite into domestic capability.

As the region watches India navigate its AI ambitions, Singapore's continued focus on quality over quantity in AI development—emphasizing practical applications, talent development, and strategic partnerships—may well prove to be a winning formula.
