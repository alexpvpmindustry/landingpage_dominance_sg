Singapore Launches New Tripartite Council to Shield Workers from AI Disruption

Date: April 30, 2026

In a decisive move to address mounting concerns about artificial intelligence's impact on employment, Singapore has established a new tripartite council dedicated to helping workers retain their jobs while enabling companies to transform amid AI disruption. The council, announced this week, brings together government, industry, and union representatives to coordinate comprehensive workforce transition support across all sectors.

A Coordinated Response to AI-Driven Change

The new council aims to coordinate efforts to reach out to more workers and companies in every sector of the Singapore economy. With AI adoption accelerating across industries—from finance to manufacturing, healthcare to logistics—officials recognized that piecemeal approaches would be insufficient to manage the scale of transformation ahead.

"This is about ensuring no worker gets left behind," said a Ministry of Manpower spokesperson. "AI is reshaping every industry, and we need a unified, proactive approach to help Singaporeans adapt to these changes."

Key Focus Areas

The council will focus on several critical areas:

Skills Retraining: Developing and scaling programs to help workers acquire new digital skills that complement rather than compete with AI systems. This includes partnerships with SkillsFuture Singapore and vocational training institutes to offer accelerated courses in areas like data analysis, AI prompt engineering, and digital literacy.

Job Matching: Creating better pathways between displaced workers and emerging job opportunities in AI-augmented roles. The council will work closely with the Employment Agencies Licensees Association and online job platforms to ensure smooth transitions.

Company Transformation Support: Providing guidance and resources to help businesses—especially SMEs—adopt AI in ways that enhance rather than replace human workers. This includes subsidies for AI implementation that prioritize human-AI collaboration.

Social Safety Nets: Reviewing and strengthening unemployment benefits and income support mechanisms for workers directly impacted by AI-driven job displacements.

Lessons from Global AI Workforce Reports

The timing of the council's launch comes on the heels of concerning global findings. A recent Morgan Stanley report revealed that AI-exposed firms globally cut 4% of jobs on average, with the automotive sector experiencing up to 10% net job losses. However, the same study found that companies investing in retraining—such as US firms with 32% staff retraining rates—fared significantly better in managing workforce transitions.

Singapore's approach appears to mirror these lessons. The new council's emphasis on proactive engagement, rather than reactive support, positions the country to avoid the more severe workforce disruptions seen in other economies.

Business Community Responds

Business groups have welcomed the initiative. The Singapore Business Federation noted that the council provides a much-needed platform for dialogue between employers, unions, and policymakers. "Companies are keen to adopt AI to stay competitive, but they also want to do so responsibly," said an FB representative. "This council gives us the framework to do exactly that."

Small and medium enterprises, which often lack resources for comprehensive AI transition planning, stand to benefit particularly from the council's coordinated guidance. Many SMEs have expressed concerns about keeping pace with AI adoption while maintaining workforce stability.

What's Next

The council is expected to unveil its first set of programs within the next three months, targeting sectors most immediately affected by AI disruption. These will likely include retail, where AI-powered checkout and inventory systems are becoming common; financial services, where algorithmic trading and robo-advisors are transforming traditional roles; and logistics, where warehouse automation continues to expand.

PM Wong is also set to address the topic at the upcoming May Day Rally, where he will speak on helping workers adapt to evolving roles due to AI and new technologies. This underscores the government's commitment to placing workforce adaptation at the center of Singapore's AI strategy.

For Singapore's workers, the message is clear: the government is proactively investing in their future, not waiting for AI disruption to hit before responding. Whether this approach will prove successful in navigating one of the most significant workforce transformations in modern history remains to be seen—but Singapore is clearly positioning itself as a leader in responsible AI adoption.
