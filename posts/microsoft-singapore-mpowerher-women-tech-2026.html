Microsoft Singapore MPowerHer: Bridging the Gender Gap in AI

Date: April 11, 2026

In a move that signals growing recognition of diversity's role in AI development, Microsoft Singapore has announced a new MPowerHer collaboration aimed at upskilling women in technology and artificial intelligence. The initiative, launched on April 9, 2026, seeks to equip thousands of Singaporean women with the skills needed to thrive in an AI-driven economy.

Why MPowerHer Matters Now

Singapore's AI sector has been expanding rapidly, with job postings for AI-related roles growing by over 60% year-over-year. Yet women remain significantly underrepresented in the field. Industry estimates suggest that women hold fewer than 25% of AI technical roles in Singapore, a gap that MPowerHer aims to address.

"AI is reshaping every industry, from finance to healthcare to logistics," said a Microsoft Singapore spokesperson. "We believe the best AI solutions come from diverse teams. MPowerHer is our commitment to ensuring Singaporean women aren't just participants in this transformation—they're leaders."

Program Details

The MPowerHer initiative will provide free training in cloud computing, machine learning, and AI fundamentals through Microsoft's Learn platform. The program targets three key groups:

  • University students: Current students pursuing STEM degrees will receive specialized AI workshops and mentorship from Microsoft engineers.
  • Career switchers: Women currently in non-tech roles who want to transition into AI-related positions will access intensive bootcamps and certification pathways.
  • Working professionals: Women already in tech roles will receive advanced training to move into AI-specialized positions.

The program aims to train 5,000 women by the end of 2027, with participants receiving certifications recognized by employers across Singapore's tech ecosystem.

Singapore's Broker Role

The timing of MPowerHer coincides with increased attention on AI talent development in Singapore. The Ministry of Digital Development and Information recently highlighted the need to build a robust pipeline of AI talent to support the country's ambition to become a regional AI hub.

Singapore's SkillsFuture program has also expanded its AI-related subsidies, making it easier for workers to reskill. Microsoft's MPowerHer adds to this ecosystem, bringing global curriculum standards and industry-recognized certifications to the table.

Industry Response

Local tech companies have welcomed the initiative. Several Singapore-based startups and large enterprises have already committed to hiring MPowerHer graduates, creating a direct pathway from training to employment.

"We've seen the difference diverse teams make in AI development," noted a HR director at a local fintech company. "Programs like MPowerHer don't just help women—they help companies build better AI products."

How to Join

Registration for MPowerHer is now open through Microsoft's Singapore portal. The program offers both online and in-person components, with dedicated learning communities to support participants throughout their journey.

For women interested in AI careers—or anyone passionate about closing the gender gap in technology—MPowerHer represents a significant opportunity. With tech giants like Microsoft leading the charge, Singapore's AI landscape could look notably different in just a few years.
